Tomato Late Blight

Late blight is a serious fungal-like disease affecting tomato and potato plants, caused by the pathogen Phytophthora infestans. It spreads rapidly in cool, wet weather and can destroy an entire field within days if left untreated.

Symptoms: Look for irregular, water-soaked dark green or brown patches on leaves, usually starting at the leaf edges. A pale green to yellow ring often surrounds the lesion. In humid conditions, a white fuzzy mold may appear on the underside of affected leaves. Stems can develop dark brown lesions, and fruit may show firm, dark, greasy-looking spots.

Conditions that favor the disease: cool temperatures (60-70F / 15-21C), high humidity above 90%, prolonged leaf wetness from rain, dew, or overhead irrigation, and dense plant canopies with poor air circulation.

Treatment and management: Remove and destroy infected plant material immediately, do not compost it. Apply a copper-based fungicide or a fungicide containing chlorothalonil as a preventive measure before symptoms appear, especially during forecasted cool wet periods. Improve air circulation by spacing plants further apart and staking or caging them. Avoid overhead watering; water at the base of the plant instead. Rotate crops and avoid planting tomatoes or potatoes in the same soil in consecutive seasons.

Prevention tips: Choose blight-resistant tomato varieties where available. Monitor local disease forecasts during the growing season. Inspect plants regularly, especially after rain.
